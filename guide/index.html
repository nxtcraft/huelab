Colour Harmony Types

  • Complementary: Two colours opposite on the wheel. High contrast — good for call-to-action.
  • Analogous: Three adjacent colours. Harmonious, calm — good for backgrounds and UI.
  • Triadic: Three colours 120° apart. Vibrant, balanced.
  • Split-complementary: One base + two adjacent to its complement. Less harsh than complementary.
  • Tetradic: Four colours forming a rectangle on the wheel. Rich but needs careful balance.
  • Monochromatic: Variations of one hue (tints, shades, tones). Clean and professional.

WCAG Contrast Ratios

Use HueLab's contrast checker to ensure your text is readable:

  • WCAG AA (minimum): 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (18pt+ or 14pt bold+).
  • WCAG AAA (enhanced): 7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text.
  • Black on white = 21:1 (maximum contrast). White on white = 1:1 (invisible).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between HEX, RGB and HSL?

HEX (#ff5733) = base-16 for web. RGB (255, 87, 51) = red/green/blue channels 0–255. HSL (11°, 100%, 60%) = hue/saturation/lightness — more intuitive for colour variations. All represent the same colour; use whichever your tool needs.

What is WCAG colour contrast?

WCAG defines minimum contrast ratios for readable text. AA: 4.5:1 normal, 3:1 large. AAA: 7:1 normal. HueLab shows pass/fail for both standards. Essential for accessible web design.

What are complementary and analogous colours?

Complementary = opposite on the colour wheel (high contrast). Analogous = adjacent colours (harmonious). Triadic = three equally spaced (120° apart). HueLab generates all these from any base colour.
